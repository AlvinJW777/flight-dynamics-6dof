Portfolio Project 02 · Flight Mechanics

Six Degrees of Freedom

Build a nonlinear rigid-body aircraft simulator from the equations of motion, trim it, linearise it, extract its natural modes — and show they match forty-year-old NASA flight data.

ToolPython — no licence, no caps
Effort30–35 h
BenchmarkNASA CR-2144
ModelAI-assisted; you own the judgement

Module brief

You are going to write, from the equations of motion up, a simulator that flies an aircraft in six degrees of freedom. Then you will find the control settings that hold it in steady flight, linearise the nonlinear model about that condition, pull the eigenvalues out of the resulting state matrix, and identify the five classical modes hiding in them — short period, phugoid, dutch roll, roll subsidence, spiral.

Then you will show your numbers match NASA's.

Learning outcomes

  1. Define and transform between Earth, body, stability and wind axes without confusing yourself.
  2. Explain why quaternions are used for attitude and what specifically fails without them.
  3. Derive and implement the 6-DOF rigid-body equations, including the rotating-frame terms.
  4. Solve trim as a nonlinear root-finding problem, and say what it means when it fails to converge.
  5. Linearise a nonlinear system numerically and know how to choose the perturbation size.
  6. Extract natural frequency and damping from eigenvalues, and identify which mode is which.
  7. Separate verification from validation and evidence each independently.

Why this, and not the CFD project

Indicative UK market counts: ~1,783 aerospace engineer roles, ~152 CFD engineer, ~70 aerodynamics engineer. Aerodynamics is about 4% of the market, contested by aero MScs and PhDs — and "did a CFD project on an aerofoil in Ansys" is the most common line on an aerospace graduate CV. You had already done a version at university.

This project is harder in the ways that count. Nothing does the work for you. There is no solver to click through: you write the equations of motion, you get the sign conventions right or the aircraft flies backwards, you solve trim yourself. And it compounds what already makes you unusual — the estimation and numerical-methods depth from the dissertation, the V&V discipline, the Python.

Two projects that tell one story — "I model dynamic systems and I prove my models are right" — beat four that tell none.

The verification and validation strategy

This is the most important design decision in the project, so it comes before any code. It mirrors what made the asteroid work defensible: two independent references answering two different questions.

QuestionAnswered byType
Did I implement the maths correctly?Your numerically-linearised model must reproduce the analytical linear model built directly from the published derivatives.Verification
Does the model describe a real aircraft?Your computed eigenvalues must match the mode frequencies and damping tabulated in NASA CR-2144.Validation

Why CR-2144 is close to ideal. Heffley and Jewell's Aircraft Handling Qualities Data (NASA CR-2144, December 1972) publishes, for ten aircraft across ten flight conditions: mass and inertia, aerodynamic derivatives, control characteristics — and the resulting transfer functions and handling-qualities parameters. It gives you both the inputs and the reference outputs.

That is exactly the structure that made the CFD benchmark valuable, and it lets you separate two failures that otherwise look identical:

  • Your linearised A matrix disagrees with the analytical one → your implementation is wrong. Sign convention, a missing rotating-frame term, a units error.
  • It agrees, but the eigenvalues miss CR-2144's modes → your implementation is fine and the model or the data is the limitation. That is a result, not a mistake.
Pick the aircraft deliberately

Start with the Boeing 747. It is in CR-2144, it is the standard worked example in Etkin & Reid and most flight dynamics texts, and it is therefore cross-referenced in more places than anything else — so when your phugoid damping looks odd you have several independent sources to check against, not one. Exotic choices leave you alone with your bug.

01

Frames & rotations

4–5 h

Almost every bug in a 6-DOF simulator is a frame or sign error, and they are hard to find because the aircraft still flies — just wrongly. Get this unit right and the rest is bookkeeping.

FrameAxesUsed for
Earth / NEDNorth, East, DownPosition, navigation. Treated as inertial.
BodyNose, right wing, downEquations of motion, inertia tensor, rate gyros
WindAlong relative windLift and drag are defined here
StabilityBody rotated by αStability derivatives are usually quoted here

Why quaternions

Euler angles have a singularity at θ = ±90°: at vertical pitch, roll and yaw become indistinguishable and the kinematic equations divide by cos θ → 0. That is gimbal lock. A quaternion has no singularity and needs four numbers with one constraint (unit norm) rather than three with a hole in them.

Checkpoint — tests that must pass before Unit 2
  • Direction cosine matrix is orthogonal: C·Cᵀ = I to 10⁻¹⁴
  • det(C) = +1 — a rotation, not a reflection. A determinant of −1 means you have mirrored the aircraft.
  • Quaternion → DCM → quaternion round-trips to itself
  • Quaternion norm stays 1 through 10⁶ integration steps (renormalise, and record how much drift you were correcting)
  • Known rotations: 90° yaw maps body-x to Earth-y; 90° pitch maps body-x to Earth-down
02

Equations of motion

6–7 h

Twelve states: three velocities, three body rates, four quaternion components, three positions.

force, body axes — note the rotating-frame coupling terms
u̇ = rv − qw − g·sinθ + Fx/m
v̇ = pw − ru + g·cosθ·sinφ + Fy/m
ẇ = qu − pv + g·cosθ·cosφ + Fz/m

moment — the ω × Iω term is where sign errors hide
ω̇ = I⁻¹ · ( M − ω × (I·ω) )

attitude kinematics
q̇ = ½ · Ω(ω) · q

navigation
ṗNED = Cbody→NED · vbody

The rv − qw terms are not optional extras — they are what makes the body frame non-inertial. Omit them and the aircraft will still fly, just not like an aircraft. This is the single most common silent error in a first 6-DOF implementation.

Checkpoint — physics that must hold regardless of implementation
  • Newton's first law: zero forces and moments → velocity constant, attitude constant, forever.
  • Free fall: gravity only → downward acceleration exactly g, no rotation.
  • Angular momentum: no external moments → |I·ω| conserved in the inertial frame.
  • Energy: no thrust, no drag → total energy conserved to integrator tolerance over a ballistic arc.
  • Dzhanibekov / intermediate axis: spin about the intermediate principal axis is unstable, the other two stable. A genuinely searching test of the ω × Iω term, and it either works or it very obviously does not.
03

Atmosphere, aerodynamics, thrust

4–5 h

ISA atmosphere: temperature lapse to 11 km, isothermal above, giving density and speed of sound as functions of altitude. Validate against the published ISA table at 0, 5, 11 and 20 km — it is tabulated everywhere and agreement should be to four significant figures.

Aerodynamics from CR-2144's stability derivatives, built as a linear expansion about the reference condition. Thrust as a simple model — that is honest at this fidelity, and you state it.

Derivative conventions will cost you a day

Stability derivatives are quoted in several conventions: dimensional or non-dimensional, per radian or per degree, in stability axes or body axes, and with rate derivatives sometimes normalised by c/2V or b/2V. CR-2144 states which it uses — read that section, do not assume. A factor of 57.3 or of 2V/c in the wrong place produces a model that trims fine and has completely wrong modes.

04

Trim

4–5 h

Trim is the flight condition where the aircraft is in equilibrium — accelerations zero, so it holds its state indefinitely. For straight and level flight, find the angle of attack, elevator deflection and throttle that make u̇ = ẇ = q̇ = 0.

That is a nonlinear root-finding problem in three unknowns. Use scipy.optimize.root or a least-squares cost.

Checkpoint
  • Residual accelerations below 10⁻¹⁰ — not "small", converged.
  • Propagate the trimmed state for 60 s with controls fixed and confirm nothing moves. This is the real test: a trim that is not actually an equilibrium will drift, and this catches it immediately.
  • Trimmed α is physically sensible — a few degrees in cruise, not 30, not negative.
  • Lift ≈ weight, thrust ≈ drag, to within a fraction of a percent.
When trim will not converge

Do not just tighten tolerances or change the initial guess until something comes out. Non-convergence usually means the flight condition is not achievable with the model as built — insufficient thrust, or the aero model extrapolated outside its valid range. Diagnose it, do not brute-force it. Understanding why a solver failed is worth more in an interview than the answer it eventually gave.

05

Linearisation

4–5 h

Perturb each state and control in turn about trim, measure the response, and assemble the Jacobians A = ∂f/∂x and B = ∂f/∂u. Use central differences.

Choosing the perturbation size

The classic trade-off, and worth understanding rather than copying. Too large and you capture nonlinearity; too small and floating-point cancellation dominates. For central differences the truncation error goes as h² and the round-off as ε/h, so the optimum is near

h ≈ ε1/3 ≈ (2.2×10⁻¹⁶)1/3 ≈ 6×10⁻⁶ × (state scale)

Scale per state — a perturbation appropriate for a velocity in m/s is wrong for a quaternion component. Demonstrate this: sweep h over several decades and plot the error against the analytical Jacobian. You will see the V-shaped curve with truncation on one side and round-off on the other, and the minimum where theory says it should be. That plot is worth a paragraph in the report.

Then decouple: longitudinal states (u, w, q, θ) and lateral-directional (v, p, r, φ). For a symmetric aircraft in symmetric flight these separate almost exactly — and how nearly they separate is itself a check on your implementation.

Checkpoint — this is the verification gate

Build the analytical A matrix directly from CR-2144's dimensional derivatives, independently of your simulator. Your numerically-linearised A must reproduce it element by element to within a fraction of a percent. If it does not, the disagreement tells you exactly which term is wrong — the matrix element points straight at the equation.

06

Modes & validation

4–5 h

Eigenvalues of A. For each complex pair, natural frequency and damping ratio:

ωn = |λ|,    ζ = −Re(λ) / |λ|
ModeAxisCharacterPhysical meaning
Short periodLongitudinalFast oscillation, moderately dampedPitch rotation at near-constant speed
PhugoidLongitudinalSlow oscillation, lightly dampedExchange of kinetic and potential energy
Dutch rollLateralOscillation, lightly dampedCoupled yaw and roll
Roll subsidenceLateralFast real rootRoll rate damping out
SpiralLateralVery slow real rootOften slightly divergent — and that is normal
An independent check you can do on paper

Lanchester's phugoid approximation says the phugoid frequency depends only on trim speed:

ωn ≈ √2 · g / V   →    period T ≈ 2πV/(√2·g) ≈ 0.45 · V seconds

So an aircraft trimmed at 250 m/s should show a phugoid period near 113 s. This drops out of energy exchange alone — no derivatives, no matrix — so it is a genuinely independent check on your eigenvalue extraction. If your phugoid period is wildly off this, look for the error before comparing to anything else.

Validation

Compare each mode's frequency and damping against CR-2144's tabulated values. Report the percentage difference per mode. Then explain the disagreements — candidate causes include the quasi-steady aerodynamic assumption, neglected unsteady derivatives, flexible-airframe effects absent from a rigid-body model, and the accuracy of the derivatives themselves.

Expect the phugoid to agree worst. It is lightly damped and sensitive to drag modelling, so a few percent on drag moves it a lot. Saying that in advance, and then showing it, reads far better than discovering it.

07

Control — stretch

4–6 h

Only if Units 0–6 are finished and documented. A pitch-attitude hold and altitude hold, tuned against the modes you just identified — which is the point: you are no longer guessing gains, you are placing them relative to known dynamics. Then a discrete gust response.

Do not start this until the rest is done

Your own plan is roughly 2× over on time. A complete, validated, well-documented simulator with no controller beats a half-documented one with a PID bolted on.

08

Write-up

4–5 h

Same document set as the asteroid project. The three figures that carry it:

  1. Pole map — your eigenvalues against CR-2144's, on the complex plane, all five modes labelled.
  2. Perturbation-size sweep — the V-curve showing truncation and round-off error, with the theoretical optimum marked.
  3. Time response — the aircraft excited in each mode, showing the character you claim (fast damped pitch, slow energy exchange, coupled yaw-roll).

Milestone 3 is the real gate

A trimmed state that will not hold for 60 seconds is not trimmed, and everything downstream — linearisation, eigenvalues, validation — is computed about a point that does not exist. Cheapest error detector in the project. Use it before believing anything else.
